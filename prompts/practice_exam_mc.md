## Multiple choice: item-writing rules
(Haladyna, Downing & Rodriguez 2002, 31 guidelines; NBME item-writing guide: test-wiseness flaws.)

- 4 options (A-D), each on its own line as `A. ...`, exactly one correct, each a single complete idea.
  The stem alone poses a clear question; put shared words in the stem, not in every option. Phrase stems
  positively (if a NOT is unavoidable, write it in capitals).
- **Answer key: use exactly this key:** {mc_key}
  Decide the correct content first, then place it at the assigned letter. The coordinator checks it.
- **No test-wiseness cues.** A student who doesn't know the content must not be able to pick the answer
  from the shape of the options:
  - All four options are about the same length and equally detailed. The correct one is NOT the longest,
    most qualified or most technical-sounding (the coordinator measures this); sometimes it is the
    shortest.
  - Distractors are as specific as the answer: same structure, same kind of numbers and units, same
    hedging. No obviously silly options; no "all of the above" or "none of the above"; no absolute words
    ("always", "never") only in the distractors.
  - No convergence: the answer must not be the option that shares the most elements with the others.
  - No word overlap that gives it away (the answer repeating the stem's key words while the distractors
    don't), and no grammatical mismatch between the stem and the distractors.
- Numerical options: distractors are the results of real errors (wrong unit prefix, dB vs linear, missing
  2 or pi, relative vs absolute permittivity, degrees vs radians), in ascending order where the key
  allows; otherwise in any order that keeps the assigned letter.
- Mix conceptual questions (definitions, which-is-true, cause and effect) with short calculations
  (one or two steps, about the paper's pace per mark).
- In the solutions: a line `**Correct Answer: X**`, then one line per distractor naming the error it
  represents.
- Before finalising, check each question: "could I get this from the options alone?"
